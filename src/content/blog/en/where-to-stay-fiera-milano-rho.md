During the busiest weeks at Fiera Milano Rho — Salone del Mobile, EICMA, HOST, MICAM, TuttoFood — finding a room in Milan gets hard: accommodation books out fast, and anyone searching last-minute struggles to find something convenient. Many visitors are flying in from abroad through Malpensa or Linate anyway, so it's worth looking beyond the city itself, and Piacenza is one of the most practical options.

## How long it really takes from Piacenza to Rho Fiera

From Piacenza, trains to Milano Centrale run frequently throughout the day. From there you'll need to change: the red metro line (M1) connects the center to Rho Fiera in a few stops, or on show days there's often a direct regional train straight to Rho Fiera Milano, with extra services added to cope with demand. Between the train and the onward connection, the total door-to-door time comes to roughly an hour and a half to two hours, depending on the connection and time of day. It's not around the corner, but it's a manageable trip if you plan for it from the start — worth building into your schedule for entering and leaving the fair.

## Who it works for, and who it doesn't

It makes sense if you're attending the fair for a day or two and don't need to head back to your accommodation several times a day, if you're traveling as a couple or small group and want a kitchen so you're not stuck relying on restaurants near the fairgrounds, or if you simply prefer sleeping in a quieter city and heading back at the end of the day without the crowds around Rho during peak periods. It makes less sense if you have evening plans in Milan outside train hours, or if you need to go in and out of the fair multiple times on tight schedules — in that case the travel time adds up too much, and it's better to stay closer to the fairgrounds. For more detail on moving between train and metro on show days, the [fair guide](/en/trade-fairs/) covers it in depth.

## What makes the stay practical

If you do base yourself in Piacenza, a few details matter when fair days run long and you arrive exhausted: self check-in with a smart lock means you don't have to coordinate arrival times with anyone, a stable internet connection is useful if you're still working in the evenings, and a fully equipped kitchen lets you put together a meal without having to go out again after a day of walking the stands. Traveling with a pet tends to narrow your options near Milan during fair weeks — here it's not an issue. Booking directly, with no intermediaries, also saves you something on the total cost of the stay.

## In short

- From Piacenza to Rho Fiera takes roughly an hour and a half to two hours — train plus metro, or a direct regional train on show days.
- Works well if you're based there for a day or two and don't need to go back and forth repeatedly.
- Less suited to evening plans in Milan or multiple entries/exits in the same day.
- Self check-in, a fully equipped kitchen, and free pet-friendly stays help during short, intense fair trips.
